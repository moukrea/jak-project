THE MECHANISM, AND WHY IT IS THE OWNER'S RULE VERBATIM. The engage signal is the authored offset
measured against a slowly-tracking BASELINE (PHYS-AUTH-BASE = 0.35/s), which exists to reject a
STATIC idle pose offset — Samos' stock beard sits at a flat 0.425 and Daxter's ears at 0.53-1.36,
and without the baseline those chains were suspended for good. But a baseline that keeps following
is a high-pass filter, and a high-pass filter measures the RATE OF CHANGE. Once it has caught up
with a pose that is merely being held, the excess collapses to zero, the hysteresis falls through
0.5*athr and physics takes the bone back. That was release armed on the authored channel's rate.
  release rule: pas la vitesse. Authored offset, not channel rate, ends the suspension.
Release is NEVER armed on the authored channel's speed any more, and a hold at exactly zero
velocity keeps its authority for as long as the animator maintains it.

THE FIX. The baseline is FROZEN the instant authored priority engages (jak-hd-physics.gc, the
authored block: it only integrates while `*phys-cauth*` is at or below 0.5, i.e. while PHYSICS
owns the joint). Rejecting a static idle offset is a question about a chain physics owns, and it
is answered before the hand-off; after it, the only thing that hands the bone back is the authored
pose RETURNING toward that frozen reference — the animator letting go. Release therefore keys on
the persistent offset between the authored pose and the physics rest, exactly as the owner
specified, and not on any velocity, counter or timeout.
