2. THE PROOF — arm64 camera matrix is bit-comparable to x86 golden (numerical REFUTED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Method: pad_replay indexes recorded input by the deterministic *display* actual-frame
counter, anchors at *target* spawn (Geyser via F1 warp), forces every RNG to a fixed
seed AND forces *ticks-per-frame* high so the engine's time-ratio pins to 1.0 every
frame. So the SAME demo produces a deterministic, fps-independent simulation on both
backends; the ONLY remaining cross-backend variable is arm64-vs-x86 floating point.

Controlled demo: rest[0..299] -> steady right-stick pan[300..1199] -> rest[1200..1499]
(Jak stationary, open plateau). x86 golden captured headless (--no-display; the
camera logic runs on the EE/GOAL thread, no GPU needed). Both traces at
evidence/replay_{x86_golden,arm64_device}.trace; demo at replay_demo_*.inputs.

Findings (evidence/replay_diff_summary.txt):
  * During the actual pan (frames ~301-343) the two backends TRACK each other to
    ~1e-4 deg; the divergence grows glacially by accumulated rounding.
  * Matched steady window 340..1180: max |device-vs-x86 yaw| = 0.0068 deg;
    max |forward-vector component diff| = 1.0e-4. Each backend's OWN per-frame yaw
    step <= 0.013 deg, sd 0.0008 deg.
  * At settled equilibria (e.g. frames 500-529) x86 is bit-EXACTLY constant
    (yaw -117.69785, forward-x -0.8815761 every frame); arm64 limit-cycles between two
    poses differing by ~1-2 ULP (yaw -117.70294 / -117.70295). Real, arm64-specific,
    but ~0.00001 deg — SUB-VISIBLE by ~5 orders of magnitude.
  * Researcher confirmation that this is EXPECTED: goalc never emits FMLA; the camera
    slerp/matrix run through JIT GOAL asm as unfused MUL+ADD on BOTH backends; sqrt is
    full-precision on both; FPCR/MXCSR both hardware-default. The camera path does NOT
    go through any mips2c leaf. So the camera math is bit-agreeing by construction; the
    only ULP wobble is the smoother failing to reach a bit-exact fixed point on arm64.

Conclusion: there is NO macroscopic arm64 per-frame camera-MATRIX numerical
divergence. The pivot hypothesis is REFUTED. Under a constant timestep the arm64
camera is smooth and matches the golden.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE REAL ROOT — integer time-ratio (k) quantization of the camera (a TIMING mechanism)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Frame-clock chain (all arm64/Android runtime + engine, quoted from source):
  * gk_android_main.cpp a35_gfps_frame_tick: k = round(real_dt * target_fps - emitted),
    clamped [1,4]; advances the virtual EE timer to the middle of k's band so the
    engine resolves exactly k. k is driven by REAL wall-clock dt, NOT by any present
    cadence.
  * drawable.gc display-frame-start: time-ratio = floor(timer/ticks-per-frame)+1 with a
    PC "<1.3 -> 1.0" snap; display.gc set-time-ratios: time-adjust-ratio and
    seconds-per-frame are proportional to that integer ratio.
  * camera-h.gc / cam-states.gc: camera velocity/position integration and the pan
    rot-speed are multiplied by time-adjust-ratio (== ratio == k). So per rendered
    frame the camera's angular advance is proportional to k.

At ~40-44fps with target_fps=60, real_dt*60 ~= 1.5, so round() DITHERS k between 1 and
2 every frame. The camera therefore advances +1*delta, +2*delta, +1*delta ... — an
uneven angular velocity that reads as pan judder. The SAME quantization lurch exists
for the world/Jak, but Jak's on-screen motion is slow and physics-damped, hiding it;
a camera pan sweeps the whole viewport at high angular speed, exposing it. This is the
owner's "action fluide à l'écran, caméra choppy". The PC/original is a locked 60fps:
k is a constant 1, the camera advances the same delta every frame = smooth. There is
no interpolation on PC — it simply runs the logic at an even, locked rate.

Why the numerical replay looked smooth: pad_replay forced the timestep to a CONSTANT
1.0, i.e. it removed exactly the k-dither that causes the judder. That is why it was
the right tool to isolate (and exonerate) the camera MATH, and also why it did not
reproduce the judder.

4. WHY THE PRIOR ATTEMPTS AND THE Gcamera-smooth FrameLimiter DID NOT FIX IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Gcamera-smooth FrameLimiter (android_gfx.cpp vsync): a MAX-rate cap only, period =
  1/target_fps. When render work exceeds the 16.67ms budget (Geyser ~40fps) the limiter
  never waits, real_dt floats freely, and k keeps dithering. It only helps when the
  device CAN hit 60 — hence "helped but did not fully fix".
* Attempt 1 (global fractional timestep): made ALL motion fractional -> changed the
  game clock, regressed fps, and made the WORLD choppy too. Wrong tool.
* Attempt 2 (camera-only sub-frame POSE interpolation, cam-update.gc): the concept
  (render the camera at the true sub-frame time) is the textbook fix, but the
  implementation lerped between the last two RENDERED poses by a [0,1] residual. Under
  k-dither consecutive rendered frames are k logic-frames apart (not 1), so the lerp
  span was wrong; and it interpolated the TITLE camera too (which has no logic motion),
  injecting instability into the view matrix -> the title-logo regression. It neither
  fixed gameplay nor preserved the title. Correctly REVERTED.

5. FIX OPTIONS (a genuine trade-off — owner decides the path)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The physics: you CANNOT get a smooth camera at a non-divisor, variable frame rate
without render-time interpolation. The choices are (a) hold an even cadence at a
60-divisor so k is constant, or (b) interpolate. Both are TIMING/interp mechanisms.

  OPTION A — Hold an even present cadence at the highest sustainable 60-divisor.
    Set the Android target_fps to 30 (goal_src/jak1/pc/pckernel-h.gc Android default),
    which drives BOTH the FrameLimiter period (33.3ms, now with spin headroom since the
    device renders in 22-27ms) AND the k-budget: real_dt ~ 33.3ms steady =>
    k = round(33.3ms*30) = 1 constant => time-adjust-ratio = 2.0 constant => even
    camera motion => smooth (this is exactly the constant-timestep condition my replay
    PROVED smooth). Game speed is preserved (real-time held). Trade-off: caps to 30fps.
    Risk: heavier levels that fall below 30 re-dither at the lower threshold (per-scene,
    not global). Smallest change, lowest risk, proven-smooth; owner must accept 30fps.

  OPTION A' — Adaptive stable-fps snap (best of both). Snap the frame cap to the
    HIGHEST 60-divisor (60/30/20) the device can currently sustain, with hysteresis, so
    the cadence is always an even multiple of 16.67ms (k always constant) at the best
    fps a scene allows: 60 (smooth) in light zones, 30 (smooth) in Geyser — never the
    juddery in-between. More logic + interacts with dynamic render scale; higher effort.

  OPTION B — Fixed logic timestep + correct render-time interpolation (decouple render
    from logic). The standard engine solution; smooth at high fps. But it is the angle
    attempt 2 got wrong and the owner rejected, it needs the interpolation span to
    honor the k-frame gap, and it MUST exclude scripted/title cameras to avoid the
    title regression. Highest effort/risk.

Recommendation: A' if the owner wants to keep high fps where possible; A for the
smallest guaranteed-smooth change if 30fps is acceptable. I can implement whichever
the owner selects and verify k becomes constant on-device (constant-k smoothness is
already demonstrated by the replay data).
