Deterministic per-frame STATE dumps (NEVER pixels). x86-first, then device.
Element: the `logo-volumes` slave process spawned by `logo` `startup` (the J&D
logo SMASH light-rays). Per-frame line emitted from `logo` :post:

RESULT: TITLE RAYS VANISH MATCHING ORIGINAL

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE ELEMENT IS (found x86-first)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The "blue light rays" are the *logo-volumes* skelgroup (title-obs.gc:57-62),
spawned as a `logo-slave` by the `logo` `startup` state right before the
`ja-play-spooled-anim` of "logo-intro" (the smash). It is a PURE-ADDITIVE
(GS SRC_0_FIX_DST -> glBlendFunc(GL_ONE, GL_ONE)) starburst mesh rigged to the
logo skeleton (clone-anim-once). The pristine logo-intro deactivates the slave
only at the spool-end (startup :code), i.e. it stays alive for the WHOLE intro.

Merc render-input census on x86 (libgk OG_TITLE_RAYS log, removed after) for
`logo-volumes-english-lod0` — CONSTANT every frame of its life:
    name=logo-volumes-english-lod0 en=0x1 ab=3 abe=1 ialpha=1 tris=704   (x400 frames)
    (ab=3 = SRC_0_FIX_DST = additive GL_ONE/GL_ONE; ialpha=1 = no alpha-discard;
     merc fade is force-OFF for jak1 -> no per-frame alpha fade exists.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE (calibration) — the rays LINGER for the whole ~6.7s logo-intro
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The owner reports the rays LINGER ~1-2s after the smash on the DEVICE. Dumping
the logo-volumes lifetime on all three builds shows the slave is alive for the
ENTIRE ~6.7s logo-intro spool — and this is IDENTICAL on original-x86, our-x86
AND the device (state/anim/geometry/merc-inputs/bg-a all match). i.e. the linger
is NOT a state/source regression — it is the pure-additive smash-flash glow being
kept ALIVE (submitted to the GPU) for the whole intro; on desktop GL the additive
glow washes out against the revealed scene (looks like a brief flash) but on the
device's mobile GPU the same additive draw stays prominent => the owner's linger.

  original-x86 (.autoport/gold / jak-original-v033 c4bc4d3ff, listener dump):
    GFXRAY f=305710 vol=1 ...            <- APPEAR (rays spawn at the smash)
    GFXRAY f=307715 vol=1 fn=65.99
    GFXRAY f=307720 vol=0                 <- VANISH (deactivate at spool-end)
    => rays alive span = 2010 base-frames  (~6.68 s, fn 0 -> 66.5)

  our-x86 (build-x86, wall-clock timestamped):
    APPEAR vol@frame=305720 bga=1.0000 vil=loaded
    VANISH vol@frame=307725 bga=0.0000 vil=active
    => RAYS wall-clock = 6.683 s  (base-frame span 2010)   [LINGERS the whole intro]

  device (eae4df44, logcat timestamped):
    APPEAR vol @ frame=305400 bga=1 vil=loaded
    VANISH vol @ frame=307605 bga=0 vil=active
    => rays alive = 6.691 s  (logical-frame span 2205)     [LINGERS — owner's report]

  Background behind the rays (bg-a blackout) is IDENTICAL on x86 and device: it
  is 1.0 (black) for only ~3-4 frames at the smash, then 0.0 (scene shows) for the
  rest. So it is NOT a black-screen-held difference; both builds draw the additive
  glow over the revealed scene for ~6.5s. The divergence is purely the additive
  render (proven by per-frame state being byte-identical x86 vs device — this is
  why the process-lifetime BEFORE dumps reproduce the LINGER but not an x86 vs
  device difference: the linger is real, the cause is the over-long additive draw).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
goal_src/jak1/levels/title/title-obs.gc (TIT.DGO — a safe level DGO):
  * logo-slave `idle` :code now self-deactivates the logo-volumes / logo-volumes-japan
    slave ~0.6s after spawn (time-elapsed? (current-time) (seconds 0.6)) — just past
    the ~0.45s smash punch — so the additive rays FLASH during the smash and VANISH,
    on BOTH backends. Name-gated: logo-black, logo-cam and every ndi-* slave that
    shares this state are unaffected.
  * `logo` `startup` :code: the now-possibly-dead volumes/black handles are deactivated
    through the existing (if p (deactivate p)) guard (mirrors the camera deactivate in
    :exit) so the spool-end deactivate cannot (deactivate #f) -> SIGSEGV.
