## Frame decode (repro12 registers)
- Post-RET SP=0x1a7ed0 = *kernel-dram-stack* top (0x1a7ee0) − 16: the consumed 16-byte
  frame sat at 0x1a7ec0..0x1a7ecf — the OUTERMOST frame band of target's thread, where
  enter-state branch-3 (gstate.gc:355-386) resets SP and pushes the
  return-from-thread-dead HOST address.
- The restored pair {x29=0x14fd24, x30=0x1d7b30} is two ZERO-EXTENDED GOAL values
  ({s7/#f, *camera-combiner*+0x6c — inside the combiner process object's
  connection-list area}). Legit saved regs are host-based (cf. live x14=0x7f0014fd24
  = host s7), so the frame was OVERWRITTEN by a 16-byte store of GOAL data
  ({symbol, object-ptr} pair — connection-node-shaped) through a bad pointer.
- GRV-CANARY (commit after 29534fe1a) watches this exact band per kernel dispatch and
  logs ANOMALY writes with tick resolution to bracket the writer.

## Canary findings (crateC2, canary v1)
- [target-stack-top−24] held host return-from-thread-dead (0x7f0018aef4) and then took
  per-tick float writes (0x3f79..-0x3f7b.. ≈ 0.973-0.984) — initially alarming, but the
  dram arena (*kernel-dram-stack* top = 0x19ab80 on that boot) is SHARED by post
  threads (gkernel.gc:1389,1972), so bare spill churn there is legitimate reuse.
- CRITICAL CORRECTION from this: repro12's crash SP (GOAL 0x1a7ed0) is NOT in the dram
  arena at all (arena ends 0x19ab80) — the consumed stack belongs to some OTHER
  process' embedded stack. Forensics v4 (GRV-POOL) now walks the active tree at crash
  time and flags the span containing SP.
