================================================================================
Owner reject (2026-06-30, attempt 1): LOWERING worked, but RAISING the scale back
up did NOT at a vsync-capped target. With target = 60 (= the 60Hz panel cap) the fps
sits at 57/58/59/60 and NEVER exceeds 60, so attempt-1's "raise only when fps >
target + margin" condition was UNSATISFIABLE -> the scale got STUCK at the lowest it
ever reached (the floor) even with plenty of GPU headroom. (At target 45 it recovered
only because the game ran well above 45, i.e. real fps-headroom above target.)

THE FIX: raise on FRAME-TIME headroom, not on "fps above target". At a vsync cap the
fps saturates and carries no headroom information, so the controller now measures the
actual render WORK TIME per frame and compares it to the per-target vblank budget. It
raises OPPORTUNISTICALLY (probing) whenever the frame work is comfortably under budget
AND fps is still meeting target within a small tolerance — even while fps == the cap.

2. THE ADAPTIVE ALGORITHM — attempt 2 (FRAME-TIME headroom + probing; anti-thrash)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO measured signals, both meaningful at a vsync cap:
  * pc-get-fps          -> smoothed presented fps (SATURATES at the cap).
  * pc-get-frame-busy-us -> smoothed render WORK time (does NOT saturate at the cap).
Per-target vblank budget = 1000 / dyn-target-fps ms (e.g. target 60 -> 16.6ms,
target 30 -> 33.3ms). Both signals get a 2nd-stage EMA on top of the C++ EMAs.

 OFF  -> returns the manual render-scale; live dynamic scale kept primed at 100 so a
         later enable starts native. Controller idle, zero overhead.

 ON, in-game:
   LOWER (real load only): fps < target-1 AND render-work >= 0.98*budget AND >=0.5s
       since last change AND scale > floor -> scale -= 7%, clamp floor, arm a >=2s
       raise-lockout. The frame-time gate is the OTHER half of the fix: it stops
       vsync JITTER (fps reads a touch under a capped target while the GPU has plenty
       of headroom) from spuriously lowering.
   RAISE (the fix — opportunistic probe on FRAME-TIME headroom, NOT fps>target):
       scale < 100 AND fps >= target-5 (so a 3-4 fps dip at a 60 cap does NOT block it)
       AND render-work <= 0.80*budget (clear headroom) AND >=1s since last change AND
       >=2s clear of the last lower -> scale += 5%. Each raise is a PROBE: the next
       cycle re-measures; if work crossed budget / fps dropped under target the LOWER
       path steps back; otherwise it keeps climbing toward 100%.
   ANTI-THRASH: 2nd-stage EMAs (act on smoothed, never single frames); a wide busy
       DEAD-BAND [0.80,0.98]*budget where nothing changes; asymmetric bounded steps
       (lower -7% fast, raise +5% slow); cooldowns + a post-lower raise lockout; floor
       clamp every frame; and a SKIP during loads/cutscenes/menus (hold + re-prime).

5. HONEST NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The render-work signal on Android is the renderer's CPU render() time (render_cpu_s);
  on this Adreno it couples with load enough to drive correct decisions (LOWER fired at
  busy 17-23ms on real fps drops; RAISE fired at busy 12.8-13.3ms with fps ~60). It is
  noisy frame-to-frame, which is why the controller acts on a 2nd-stage EMA and a wide
  dead-band, and why the equilibrium at a heavy zone wanders a few % rather than sitting
  on one exact value. The MIDDROP climb demo (B) isolates the raise from that noise.
- At target=60 in a permanently heavy zone the scale settles LOW (low-40s) because
  60fps there genuinely requires a low scale; that is the feature (trade sharpness for
  the target), not the bug. The fix is that it now RAISES whenever headroom appears
  (proven by the 5 raises in A at fps~60 and the clean 51->76% climb in B), instead of
  attempt-1's monotonic sink-and-stick at the floor.
- 0.49s min adjustment gap is marginally under the 0.5s nominal lower cooldown (timer
  rounding during the rapid spike cascade); steady-state cadence is multi-second.
