== PER-TEXTURE NORMAL-MAP SPACE CENSUS (phase Gpbr-per-texture-materials) ==

NATURE  two dimensionless correlations in [-1,1] -- NOT an amplitude.
FRAME   the material's own image grid; dU = +x (columns), dV = +y (rows). Both maps are
        sampled on the same grid, so no resampling and no world units enter.
ABSENT  every material shows the SAME (sign cx, sign cy) pair. What this instrument can
        find is a MIX -- one material placing its green channel the other way round from
        its neighbour -- because a comparison of materials AGAINST EACH OTHER cancels
        every convention they share. A material whose pair differs from the majority is
        the finding; a unanimous table means a per-texture normal_y has nothing to
        correct today.
CANNOT  decide whether the SHARED convention is the one the engine assumes. That is one
        GLOBAL bit, and answering it needs the loader's upload row order and the
        orientation the level's own UVs give V -- neither is in these files. Reading a
        per-texture 'flipped' verdict off this table would be a false red.
        |cx| and |cy| both below 0.15 => the two maps are not derived from one another
        and the row says INDETERMINATE rather than inventing a verdict.

VERDICT: HOMOGENEOUS. Every material places its green channel the same way relative
to its own height field, so there is NO per-texture mix and a per-texture normal_y
has nothing to repair today. That is a real result and not an absence of one: it
REMOVES 'some textures ship a flipped green' from the candidate causes of the owner's
face-to-face inconsistency, which is what the census was run to test.
WHAT STAYS OPEN, NAMED: whether the shared convention is the one the engine assumes
is ONE GLOBAL BIT this instrument cannot reach (see CANNOT above). The knob now
exists, so testing it costs one line -- `normal_y -1` in the [defaults] block of
recharged_assets/materials.txt -- and no rebuild. It is the OWNER's eye that closes
it, not a number of mine.
