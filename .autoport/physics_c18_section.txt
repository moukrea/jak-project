THE DEFECT, FOUND AND NAMED — AND IT IS THE OWNER'S OLDEST COMPLAINT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The owner has said the same sentence for a week: "les meches de Keira sont ANCREES", "les
pans de veste NE BOUGENT PAS DU TOUT", "le col est completement statique", "la poitrine de
Keira completement statique". This cycle has the mechanism, and it is arithmetic rather
than tuning.

STEP 3b of the solver walks a chain's pose back toward the AUTHORED pose by bisection on
the blend fraction t, accepting a candidate only where the worst mesh penetration is under
PHYS-PEN-TOL (1 unit). The search starts at tsel = 0 and halves PHYS-MESH-BISECT times, so
the smallest NON-ZERO t it can ever return is 2^-PHYS-MESH-BISECT — at 6 halvings, 1/64.
Penetration is very nearly linear in t near zero, so a chain whose simulated pose
penetrates by more than about 64 units has NO admissible candidate: tsel stays exactly 0,
`phys-chain-blend!` at t = 0 reproduces the authored pose bit for bit, and the bone is
written with the animator's pose every single frame. The measured windows carried mraw =
587 on jak-hd and 232 on keira-hd, both far past that threshold.

It was invisible for the usual reason. A chain welded to the authored pose has deviation
0.0000, length ratio 1.0000, drift 0.0000 and settle time 0 — it maximises every calm
ceiling in the file, which is SPEC 7's whole lesson restated in a new place.

MEASURED BEFORE THE FIX, on a real x86 run in Sandover with the actor driven:
  keira-hd rmidhair  cdev = 0.0000  clenr = 1.0000  cvar = 0.0000 over 443 locomotion
                     frames and 2749 frames on which its own anchor was being driven
  keira-hd lmidhair  identical
  keira-hd rbang     cvar = 0.1790, effectively welded
Those are the meches. The defect is not that they moved too little; it is that the
collision clamp removed one hundred percent of their motion, every frame.

THE FIX — THE VETO BECOMES A BOUND
-----------------------------------
  1. PHYS-MESH-BISECT 6 -> 10, so a feasible blend fraction below 1/64 can actually be
     found instead of collapsing to zero.
  2. PHYS-TMIN-FLOOR = 0.35. The clamp may no longer take more than 65 % of a chain's
     displacement. SPEC 6 is explicit that "une resolution pire que le clip est pire que
     pas de resolution", and a resolution that deletes all of the motion is that case
     exactly. The owner's blocker is carried by the REAL SKINNED SURFACE pass — SPEC 18's
     designated authority — which resolves to a zero residual on its own; this
     proxy-volume bisection is the coarse net behind it and it may not veto.
  3. The diagnosis is kept live rather than thrown away with the bug: `ctz` counts, per
     chain, the frames on which the raw search came back under the floor — i.e. the frames
     the clamp WOULD have vetoed — and `ctmin` reports the worst blend fraction held. Both
     are counted BEFORE the floor is applied, so the defect stays visible after its fix.

TWO MORE DEFECTS THE UN-WELDING EXPOSED, BOTH FIXED IN THE SAME CYCLE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Un-welding the chains turned three calm numbers from "perfect" into "measurable", which is
the point of SPEC 7 and also the cost of it. Two of them were real defects hiding behind the
weld, and both are fixed here.

(A) NOTHING MAY BE CRUSHED — the length constraint had no last word.
The distance constraint runs per link and the body/surface push runs RIGHT AFTER IT on the
same link, so every push landed on a length nothing re-checked. The first run in which Jak's
chains could move at all measured anklestrapR at 0.8631 of its modelled length, footstrapL/R
and anklestrapL at ~0.958 — the boot straps jammed between the leg volume and the ground on
a landing frame. A length-restoration pass now runs after every push, moving along the
segment (the smallest correction that restores the length) and mirrored into `prev` by the
same velocity-neutral rule every other correction here uses. Collision still has the last
word: STEP 3b runs after it. Owner rule X, Jak's collar, is the named case and it reads
0.9999.

(B) FAMILY A HAD NOTHING LEFT TO PULL IT BACK TO THE MODEL POSE.
SPEC 17 deleted every suppressor including the model-pose ladder (PHYS-MODELPULL), and
nothing in the rewritten solver implements the owner's rule W: "la position idle devrait
EXACTEMENT etre celle du modele de base, pas plus haut, pas plus bas, c'est la que ca
retourne naturellement". It read ~0 all week for the same reason as everything else — a
chain welded to the animation sits exactly on the model pose by construction. Measured once
they were free: assistant-lod0 463.6, jak-hd 420.1, samos-hd 344.7, eichar-lod0 272.4,
dax-hd 250.6 units, against a bar of 8. Two orders of magnitude, cast-wide.
SPEC 17 allows a suppressor back "QU'UN PAR UN, chacun justifie par un defaut MESURE", and
those numbers are that justification. The model-pull is re-added, family A only, gated on
the chain having ALREADY been quiet for PHYS-RESTCALM frames — so it can never remove motion
that exists, only drift that has already stopped — bounded per frame, velocity-neutral, and
carrying its own line in the displacement ledger so that if it IS eating the sim the report
says so in units rather than hiding it.

(C) A HYPOTHESIS THAT WAS TESTED AND REFUTED, AND IS THEREFORE NOT IN THIS BUILD.
`anim=excite` applies a force (anim - targ) * excite — a spring toward the ANIMATOR's
channel proportional to the STANDING offset — so a constant offset moves the chain's
EQUILIBRIUM. That is structurally the same thing `hang` is banned from family A for and that
the validator gates as FAM-bis, and it looked like the explanation for the model-pose
deviation: Daxter's ears carry excite and settled 4.6 and 6.1 cm off the model pose, while
his tail, on the same actor and the same frames, sat at 0.1 cm.
IT WAS MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSUMED, and the measurement said no. Moving all 40 family-A
excite chains across 18 models to anim=replace changed dax-hd's restdevA from 250.5582 to
250.5104 — nothing — while it cost sage-lod0's earL and assistant-lod0's chestR enough
liveliness to drop them under the inertness bar. A change that does not do what it was
adopted for, and takes motion with it, is exactly the kind this phase has been paying for
all week, so it has been REVERTED and only the measurement is kept. `rootlock` on Keira's
bangs, which did what it was adopted for, stays.
The structural objection to `excite` on family A remains true and is recorded for the next
cycle with its proper fix, which is code and not data: an excitation should be the
frame-to-frame CHANGE in (anim - targ), not its level — that excites a chain from ND's
keyframes without moving where it comes to rest.

(D) KEIRA'S FRONT BANGS, rbang/lbang: rootlock 1 -> 0. Three-link chains with only two free
links, sitting where the clamp fights hardest (ctz = 2099 frames). Owner item D is this
exactly: "le degrade doit s'adapter a la LONGUEUR de la chaine — une chaine de 2 maillons
doit quand meme bouger".

THE SUPPRESSOR LEDGER (SPEC 16) — IN DISPLACEMENT, NOT IN FRAMES
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The owner rejected "% of frames a suppressor is active" as the same wrong-dimension error
as `crun`: a clamp can run on every frame and never bite, or bite on 5 % of frames —
exactly the ones where movement was about to start — and kill everything. He also said the
20 % threshold was invented, so the displacement ledger below is REPORTED and the 20 % bar
is applied only to the metric SPEC 17 actually stated it for, the share of chain-frames.

Both numbers descend from the one primary quantity. `remprod` is the displacement the
integrator produced, |out0 - targ| summed over written links and frames; `remclamp` is what
STEP 3b and the strand pass took away from it, |out0 - out|; `remauth` is how far authored
priority moved the spring target. Nothing here counts frames and nothing is derived from a
chain index.

@@SUPPRESSORS@@

THE PER-NAMED-CHAIN VERDICT — THE CHAINS THE OWNER CALLS STATIC
----------------------------------------------------------------
He named them, so they are answered by name. `cvar` is the mean per-frame variation of the
WRITTEN joint on locomotion frames — a chain welded at a constant offset scores 0 here no
matter how far from its rest pose it is welded, which is the entire point. `cinr` is the
same quantity over the frames the chain's OWN anchor was driven, and it is what the verdict
is taken on (INERT below 0.25 over at least 30 samples). `cvn`/`cish` are the sample counts:
a zero next to them is UNJUDGED, never clean.

ONE THING THIS RUN CHANGED ABOUT HOW THE NUMBERS ARE TAKEN, because it decides them: the
device drives Jak with `setprop debug.opengoal.cpad_inject`, and that TU is Android-only.
The x86 leg synthesizes a pad_replay v2 file instead (4 x 12 s of held stick, two jumps, an
eight-way turn). It matters more than it sounds: measured here, EVERY jak-hd chain reads
INERT while he stands still and MOVING once he is driven. An undriven leg would have
reported the owner's defect on chains that do not have it, and missed it on Keira, who is
an NPC and is never driven at all.

@@PERMODEL@@

THE GATE SET IS JOINTLY UNSATISFIABLE UNDER THE PRESENT COLLIDERS — SHOWN, NOT ASSERTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the finding a supervisor needs before setting the next cycle, so it is stated with
the measurements that establish it rather than as an opinion.

`PHYS-TMIN-FLOOR` is the single parameter that decides how much of a chain's displacement
the collision clamp is allowed to remove. Two gates read it in OPPOSITE directions:

  * C14-A / the inertness verdict wants the floor HIGH. A chain the clamp pins at the floor
    every frame keeps only that fraction of its motion. Measured this cycle at floor 0.35:
    sage-lod0 earL is clamped on 2999 frames of 3000 (ctz=2999) and reads cinr=0.2162
    against a bar of 0.25 — INERT. Its mirror earR, IDENTICAL in every parameter, is clamped
    on 1 frame and reads 0.4711 — MOVING. The parameters are not the difference; the clamp is.
  * W / restdevA wants the floor LOW. restdevA is the distance a settled family-A chain sits
    from the model pose, and that distance is produced by the collision PUSH. Anything that
    reduces the push's influence reduces it: at floor 0.35 dax-hd reads 250.5, and with the
    family-A proxy push removed entirely it read 157.6. At floor 0 — the old behaviour — the
    written pose IS the authored pose and restdevA is 0 by construction.

So the floor cannot be raised without more chains resting off-model, and cannot be lowered
without more chains reading INERT; at its limit it reproduces the welded build the owner has
rejected six times. There is no value of it that satisfies both gates.

WHAT BREAKS THE TIE IS NOT A PARAMETER. Both symptoms come from the same place: the
collision push is large because the volumes doing it are proxies with a median radius of 975
units (24 cm) and a maximum of 4546 (1.1 m) on a 2.3 m character. Two candidate mechanisms
were tested and RULED OUT this cycle rather than assumed:
  * "the balloons push chains off their sculpt" — WRONG. `ccap` already caps every capsule,
    per link, at the distance from that link's MODEL pose minus PHYS-MODEL-CLR, so a capsule
    cannot push a link further from the axis than its own sculpted position. Checked in the
    code, not guessed.
  * "family A should collide against the real surface only, per SPEC 18" — REFUTED BY THE
    OWNER'S OWN BLOCKER. It did improve fidelity (dax-hd restdevA 250.5 -> 157.6) and it
    broke the thing that outranks it: surfpen, the residual depth of a written link inside
    the character's REAL skinned surface, went from ~0 to 217.6 on keira-hd, 648.8 on jak-hd
    and 733.6 on dax-hd in the same run. The real-surface pass measures well but does not
    HOLD a chain out on its own. Reverted the same day.
That leaves the actual SPEC 18 work — the real surface RESOLVING, not merely auditing — as
the thing that closes both gates, and it is a cycle of its own, not a tuning pass.

WHY THIS DOES NOT PASS ITS OWN VALIDATOR, AND WHY IT WAS NOT MADE TO
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Four gates are red, and all four are red for TWO causes, both named and measured above:

  1. W — restdevA <= 8. Measured 250-526 across the cast. A family-A chain is held off the
     model pose by a PROXY CAPSULE; the within-actor control is dax-hd, whose tail settles

  2. RATCHET — restdevA, and lenmin 0.8944 on assistant-lod0 in the rider leg. Same cause.
  3. The x86 leg's own run gate — 2 of 150 owner-named chains still read INERT
     (assistant-lod0 chestR at cinr=0.1770, sage-lod0 earL at 0.2162, against a bar of
     0.25). Both are stock NPCs and both are marginal, and two hypotheses were tested on
     them this cycle and refuted by measurement rather than argued away.
  4. BLOCKER-ABS — because 3 is honestly carried in this report instead of averaged away.

THE GREEN WAS AVAILABLE AND WAS REFUSED. Every one of these numbers read clean for a week,
and it read clean because the collision clamp was writing the AUTHORED pose onto any chain
that penetrated: a chain welded to the animation has deviation 0, length ratio 1.0, drift 0
and model-pose fidelity 0 by construction. Restoring that clamp would turn all four gates
green in one line — and would restore exactly the build the owner has now rejected six
times in a row with the same sentence. That trade is the whole disease this phase has, so
the report is red and says why.

WHAT CLOSES THEM is already specified and is not a tuning pass: SPEC 18's unfinished half —
the real skinned surface, which resolves to its own zero residual today, REPLACING the proxy
capsules in the resolve instead of only auditing after them. The capsules' median radius is
975 units (24 cm) and their max is 4546 (1.1 m) on a 2.3 m character. They are the thing
holding chains off their pose, crushing them in the rider leg, and generating the veto
pressure counted in `ctz`. One cause, one fix, three gates.
